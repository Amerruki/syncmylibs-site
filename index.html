Photos opens one library. You have more than one.

SyncMyLibs is a Mac app that opens all of them in one window and moves photos between them with the faces, keywords and albums still attached.

Not released yet. It needs a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 15 Sequoia or later.

What it does

Every library, open at once

Your main library, the one Photos opens, and any others you keep, on this Mac or on an external drive, side by side in one sidebar.

Photos move with their details

Faces and people, keywords, albums and folders, locations, dates, photo edits, Live Photos and RAW+JPEG pairs come across with each photo. The original is never re-encoded.

Merges without doubles

Before one library goes into another, it finds the photos already there, exact copies or look-alikes, and asks whether to skip them.

Where it falls short

  • Faces don't carry into your main library. Apple gives apps no way to write them there, so Photos finds the faces again on its own. Copies into any other library keep them.
  • Edited videos arrive unedited. Photos keeps a video's edits as instructions, not as a finished file, so there is nothing to copy.
  • People are matched by name. A copy won't work out that an unnamed face is someone you named in the other library.
  • Albums lose their manual order. Every photo arrives, but not in the order you arranged them.
  • Renaming or merging people in your main library stays on that Mac, and Photos has to be closed while it happens.
  • It needs Full Disk Access, because macOS keeps photo libraries behind its file protections. It connects to two things only: the update check, and Apple Maps if you switch maps on, which sends a photo's location to Apple.
